# Executive research summary

V4 closes the main v1–v3 modeling gap: adults give advice from beliefs they acquired as juveniles and subsequently update. Juvenile evidence storage survives maturation; newborns learn from the current adult culture. Inherited deference and maturation time compete under shared teachers and resources. Private evidence accuracy stays fixed across candidate traits.

**Already known:** information-quality thresholds, cultural inertia, benefits/costs of extended learning, and the distinction between population abundance and individual selection have extensive precedents. The literature review verifies 13 requested anchors and 18 additional primary studies through October 2026. We claim no new elementary mechanism.

**Tested:** fixed deference, public-only compliance, memoryless storage, private learning, an oracle Bayesian one-decision benchmark, and an individual feedback-based trust learner; stable, reversed, recurring and randomly changing environments; alternative initial cultures; confidence and age-hazard sensitivities; rare-mutant and explicit mutation-selection competition.

**Observed:** under stable conditions, storing adult testimony improves future adult accuracy and population abundance, and supports an interior maturation optimum without a direct tau reward. It also increases retained confidence and delays recovery after a shock. In a six-history-per-rate follow-up, rapid environmental switching reverses the mean finite-horizon deference advantage. Most of the extra cultural effect disappears when public compliance does not write testimony into memory. Adults remain able to update; permanent dogmatism was not demonstrated.

**Evolutionary finding:** isolated abundance favors d=1 and tau≈3.19 under the defaults. The refined invasion-gradient candidate is d=1, tau≈.747. The shorter-maturation lineage invades the longer-maturation resident and replaces it in an explicit shared-culture run while equilibrium abundance falls about 6%. Its low-density growth is higher. This is evidence of selection–abundance disagreement under a specified cultural externality, not proof that selection reduced population growth. The d optimum is a boundary; numerical uninvadability over a finite domain is not an analytical ESS proof.

**Negative findings:** fixed deference has no demonstrated special advantage over adequately informed reliability-sensitive learning. Stable high deference matches the oracle; changes expose limitations of uncritical internalization. Advantages over the implemented RL learner depend on its finite trust state and feedback design. Multiple lasting cultural attractors were not established by the tested initializations. Removing all truth-linked information and demographic filtering produces chance accuracy.

**Adversarial findings that changed the analysis:** corrected nonfinite parameter acceptance, equilibrium residual timing, misleading “long-run” labels, absolute-threshold recovery confounding, oracle information access, uninformative-private controls with residual truth-linked demographic selection, and the false inference of an optimum from a coarse competition boundary. Numerical source checks, exported-data consistency checks, and offline browser interaction checks pass; verification.md records their scope and measured errors.

**Paper potential:** a reproducible, carefully delimited theoretical comparison of how retained culture changes maturation selection and demographic abundance. Stronger claims require source-lineage/correlation models, real Bayesian learners with unknown reliability, competition between learning architectures, richer normative/coordination mechanisms and empirically grounded quantities. We have not established a distinct evolutionary adaptation for childhood dogmatic deference, genetic separability from cognition, or a historical explanation of human childhood.
