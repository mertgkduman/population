# v4 model comparison: deference, retained culture, and evolving maturation

**Status:** pre-implementation model decision  
**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research confidence:** MEDIUM. The mathematical recommendations are derived directly below and the biological motivations were cross-checked against primary papers, but this remains a hypothetical model rather than a fitted account of any species.

## Decision

Implement **Candidate A**, a finite signed-score stage model, as the main model. It is the smallest model that simultaneously provides:

- an inherited juvenile deference trait `d = P(adult recommendation is chosen | private and adult recommendations conflict)`;
- a fixed private-signal accuracy that is not silently improved when `d` changes;
- an inherited maturation timescale `tau`;
- endogenous adult advice generated by the resident adult cultural distribution;
- retention of the juvenile confidence score through maturation;
- exactly two demographic stages, follower/juvenile `F` and adult `A`, with finite cultural substates inside each stage;
- density-regulated resident equilibria and a valid linear rare-mutant operator in the **same resident culture and resource environment**.

Use **Candidate C**, a calibrated one-decision source selector, and **Candidate D**, feedback-based reinforcement learning of source reliability, as the two inferential comparators. Candidate B, private-only learning, is a negative control rather than a full scientific competitor.

Candidate D is substantively different from A and C: it learns source value retrospectively from experienced feedback. It does not receive an imposed time path of adult quality, and it does not assume that the learner already knows current source accuracy.

## Quantities that must remain distinct

| Symbol | Meaning | Persistent? | Inherited? |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `E in {-1,+1}` | Current environmental state / correct action | Until a shock | No |
| `I in {-1,+1}` | Fresh private recommendation | One encounter | No |
| `S in {-1,+1}` | Transmitted adult recommendation | One encounter | No |
| `U in {-1,+1}` | Juvenile's public/endorsed action | One encounter | No |
| `Z in {-H,...,H}` | Stored signed confidence/endorsement score | Across encounters and maturation | Cultural, not genetic |
| `B(Z)` | Stored belief, the sign of the score | Across encounters | Cultural, not genetic |
| `C(Z)=abs(Z)` | Confidence depth of stored belief, optionally normalized by `H` for plots | Across encounters | Cultural, not genetic |
| `d` | Probability of choosing `S` on an `I != S` conflict | Lifetime trait | Yes |
| `tau` | Mean waiting time to maturation | Lifetime trait | Yes |
| `r in {-2,...,2}` | Learned adult-source trust in Candidate D only | Across encounters and maturation | Cultural/cognitive, not genetic |

Do not call `U` a belief, `Z` a private signal or literal Bayesian evidence, `C` an accuracy, or `d` confidence. The unit score steps are not source-specific log-likelihood ratios. A stored belief can be wrong and highly confident. A correct public action can coexist with a wrong stored belief. Those distinctions are the point of the extension.

## Candidate A — finite-score fixed-deference model (recommended main)

### Biological and cultural state

For inherited trait `g=(d,tau)`, maintain vectors

```text
F_g = (F_g,-H, ..., F_g,0, ..., F_g,H)
A_g = (A_g,-H, ..., A_g,0, ..., A_g,H)
```

There remain exactly two demographic stages per inherited trait. The `2H+1` entries are cultural/confidence substates, not extra ages or demographic stages. Maturation is exponential at rate `m=1/tau` and transfers the score without resetting it:

```text
F_g,z  --1/tau-->  A_g,z.
```

Use

```text
B(z) = sign(z),                         z != 0
P(B(0)=+1) = P(B(0)=-1) = 1/2
C(z) = |z|/H.
```

The environment is binary, `E in {-1,+1}`. Environmental shocks flip `E`; they do not relabel or reset `Z`. Therefore a shock makes previously correct, high-confidence culture stale, after which fresh private recommendations and adult updating can repair it.

### Endogenous adult advice

For an adult distribution `A`, define the adult stored-belief accuracy

```text
rho_E(z) = 1       if sign(z)=E
           0       if sign(z)=-E
           1/2     if z=0

q_A(A,E) = sum_z rho_E(z) A_z / sum_z A_z.
```

If transmission flips the sampled adult recommendation with probability `eta`, the advice arriving to a juvenile has accuracy

```text
q_adv(A,E) = (1-eta) q_A + eta (1-q_A)
             = eta + (1-2 eta) q_A.
```

This quantity is endogenous. It must be calculated from the resident adult state, never prescribed as an improving function of calendar time.

### Private signal and public choice

At each juvenile decision encounter, use the adult distribution and `q_adv` from immediately before the encounter, then draw a fresh private recommendation with fixed accuracy

```text
P(I=E)=p_I,       p_I=0.65 in the primary experiment.
```

Draw `S` from the current adult testimony distribution. Candidate A then chooses

```text
U = I = S                         if I=S
U = S with probability d         if I!=S
U = I with probability 1-d       if I!=S.
```

There is no direct reward or cost for `d`. Its immediate demographic effect is mediated only by action accuracy.

### Evidence update and the identification caveat

The implemented main mechanism stores the endorsed action as one unit of a bounded endorsement score:

```text
Z' = clip(Z + U, -H, H).
```

Call this assumption **uncritical internalization**. It is transparent and tractable, but it gives `d` two causal roles:

1. choosing the public action on a private/social conflict; and
2. changing which recommendation is written into future stored belief.

It therefore does not, by itself, isolate pure behavioral deference. A strong result for `d` in Candidate A may be caused by a cultural feedback loop that resembles openness/closure even though there is no explicit dogmatism genotype.

The following implemented ablation is consequently mandatory:

```text
Public-only / no-internalization:
  U is selected using d, but Z is updated independently of U.
```

In the current implementation, the public-only ablation stores the fresh private recommendation, `Z'=clip(Z+I,-H,H)`, while `d` still changes the public action. The learning rate, memory bound `H`, adult updating rate, and confidence rule do not vary genetically. If an evolutionary result appears only under uncritical internalization, report it as selection on a **deference–internalization package**, not as selection on deference alone.

### Adult updating

Adults retain the score acquired as juveniles. In the primary dynamic model they also receive fresh private recommendations at a fixed, nonheritable rate `nu_A`:

```text
Z --nu_A p_I-->       clip(Z+E,-H,H)
Z --nu_A (1-p_I)-->   clip(Z-E,-H,H).
```

This prevents culture from being permanently frozen and gives a measured recovery mechanism after environmental shocks. Always include `nu_A=0` as a sensitivity case; adult updating can qualitatively change recovery and the selection gradient on `d`.

### Demography without a direct maturation reward

Let accurate adult stored belief affect ecological performance, not `tau` directly:

```text
beta_z(E) = b_0 + b_gain rho_E(z)
mu_A,z(E) = mu_A,0 + k_A [1-rho_E(z)].
```

Let juvenile public-action error affect juvenile mortality:

```text
mu_F(d,q_adv) = mu_F,0 + k_F epsilon_U(d,q_adv).
```

Longer juvenile duration can then improve the adult score distribution by allowing more learning encounters, but it also delays reproduction and exposes juveniles to mortality longer. This creates a real information–life-history trade-off; do not add a direct benefit of `tau` or tune a fecundity bonus as a function of `tau`.

With `R(N)` a decreasing resource/density factor, `e_0` the unit vector placing births in `z=0`, `G_F` the juvenile score-transition generator, and `G_A` the adult private-update generator, the monomorphic resident equations are

```text
dF/dt = e_0 R(N) beta_E^T A
        + G_F(d, q_adv(A,E), p_I) F
        - (tau^-1 + mu_F) F

dA/dt = tau^-1 F
        + G_A(p_I,E) A
        - D_A(E) A,

N = 1^T(F+A),
D_A(E) = diag(mu_A,z(E)).
```

The implementation uses `S(N)=S0/(1+N/K)` and `R(N)=S(N)/(KS+S(N))`, a positive saturating resource factor. A logistic multiplier that can become negative is not used inside a birth rate.

### Matrix construction interface

For `E=+1`, let

```text
c_U(d,q_adv) = P(U=E) = p_I + d(q_adv-p_I).
```

Under uncritical internalization, `G_F` is a clipped nearest-neighbor random walk with up-event rate `nu_F c_U` and down-event rate `nu_F(1-c_U)`. Boundary events that point out of range remain in the boundary state. Build the generator by accumulating off-diagonal transition rates and setting each column diagonal to minus its outgoing transition rate. This convention makes the operator Metzler and conserves abundance under learning alone.

Under `E=-1`, reverse the meanings of up and down; do not change the numerical score labels after a shock.

## Exact analytical benchmarks

### Benchmark 1: one-decision accuracy and null

For binary recommendations, on a disagreement exactly one source is correct. Therefore, regardless of correlation between the sources,

```text
P(U=E)
  = P(I=E,S=E)
    + d P(I!=E,S=E)
    + (1-d) P(I=E,S!=E)
  = p_I + d(q_adv-p_I).
```

Thus

```text
epsilon_U(d,q_adv) = 1-p_I-d(q_adv-p_I),
d epsilon_U / dd = p_I-q_adv.
```

Immediate action selection favors higher `d` iff `q_adv>p_I`, favors lower `d` iff `q_adv<p_I`, and is exactly neutral at `q_adv=p_I`.

In the implemented mean-field model, `q_adv=p_I` is a full `d` null even with internalization: both juvenile mortality and the score-transition generator depend on `d` only through the marginal chosen-action accuracy `p_I+d(q_adv-p_I)`. This exact null relies on independent teacher redraw and on summarizing testimony by its marginal accuracy; the model does not retain teacher identity, genealogy, or private–teacher dependence across encounters.

### Benchmark 2: stationary `H=1` score walk

For a constant environment and a clipped three-state walk `z in {-1,0,+1}`, let `c=P(U=E)` and `r=c/(1-c)`. The stationary learning-only distribution is

```text
pi_- = 1/(1+r+r^2)
pi_0 = r/(1+r+r^2)
pi_+ = r^2/(1+r+r^2).
```

Its stored-belief accuracy, with random ties, is

```text
q_store = pi_+ + pi_0/2
        = (r^2+r/2)/(1+r+r^2).
```

This is a kernel test, not the stationary distribution of the full stage model, because maturation, births, and deaths reweight score states.

### Benchmark 3: collapsed two-stage eigenvalue

With cultural states suppressed and constant adult production `beta`, the low-density projection matrix is

```text
M = [ -(tau^-1+mu_F)     beta ]
    [  tau^-1           -mu_A ].
```

The dominant eigenvalue is

```text
r_+ = -[tau^-1+mu_F+mu_A]/2
      + 0.5 sqrt([tau^-1+mu_F-mu_A]^2 + 4 beta/tau).
```

The numerical operator must reproduce this value when `H=0` or when all cultural states are collapsed to identical vital rates.

## Valid rare-mutant analysis

### Resident first

For resident `g_R=(d_R,tau_R)` and fixed environment, integrate the nonlinear resident system to a stable ecological–cultural equilibrium

```text
x_R* = (F_R*,A_R*).
```

Record from this same equilibrium:

```text
q_adv,R*,     R_R*=R(N_R*),     and, if needed, the full adult testimony distribution Pi_R*(S,C).
```

### Freeze the resident environment for every mutant

For mutant `g_M=(d_M,tau_M)`, build the `2(2H+1)`-dimensional linear Metzler operator

```text
L_M = [ G_F(d_M,q_adv,R*,p_I)-(tau_M^-1+mu_F,M)I     e_0 R_R* beta_E^T ]
      [ tau_M^-1 I                                    G_A-D_A(E)          ].
```

The mutant samples resident adult testimony. It does not create a mutant-only advice pool at rarity, and mutant adults do not alter `q_adv,R*` or `R_R*` to first order. This is what makes resident and mutant share one cultural and resource environment.

Define equilibrium invasion fitness as the spectral abscissa

```text
s(g_M;g_R) = alpha(L_M) = max Re(eigenvalue(L_M)).
```

Required checks:

1. every off-diagonal entry of `L_M` is nonnegative;
2. learning-only columns conserve mass;
3. `L_R x_R*=0` after resident quantities are frozen, with a small normalized residual;
4. the primary transition graph is irreducible, or reducible boundary cases are identified explicitly;
5. self-invasion satisfies `|s(g_R;g_R)| < tolerance` independently of the residual check;
6. an independently integrated rare clone has the same initial exponential growth rate;
7. pairwise invasibility is computed from `s(g_M;g_R)`, not from isolated-population growth maxima.

If vertical transmission from a mutant biological parent is later added, its parent-to-offspring cultural coupling remains in the mutant linear operator. Oblique testimony from the population remains resident-dominated. Do not accidentally discard a first-order mutant parent effect or include a first-order mutant population-pool effect.

### Environmental shocks

A common environmental shock makes the resident culture and density time dependent. First simulate and save the resident path

```text
E(t), x_R(t), q_adv,R(t), R_R(t).
```

Then expose every mutant to that exact path:

```text
dx_M/dt = L_M(t | resident path) x_M.
```

For a periodic resident attractor of period `T`, integrate the fundamental matrix

```text
dPhi/dt = L_M(t) Phi,     Phi(0)=I
```

and compute the Floquet invasion exponent

```text
s_F = log(rho(Phi(T)))/T.
```

For a shared Markov shock path, estimate the pathwise top Lyapunov exponent after burn-in, using paired common shock sequences and QR/log-norm stabilization. A clone-normalized finite-time diagnostic is

```text
s_T = (1/T) log[
        (||x_M(T)||/||x_M(0)||)
        /
        (||x_R,probe(T)||/||x_R,probe(0)||)
      ],
```

where the resident probe is propagated linearly on the same saved path. Do not average frozen-time dominant eigenvalues, and do not interpret the dominant eigenvalue of an environment-averaged matrix as the almost-sure invasion exponent under common shocks.

## Candidate B — private-only negative control

Set `U=I`, make the stored score follow private recommendations only, and prevent adult testimony from affecting the learner. Candidate B verifies that:

- changing adult transmission error has no effect;
- `d` is inactive;
- adult culture can still arise from accumulated private recommendations and persist through maturation;
- any social-model result is not a generic consequence of score memory or density regulation.

This is a control, not one of the two main inferential comparators.

## Candidate C — calibrated one-decision source selector

On a conflict, use the more informative of the private recommendation and the possibly inverted adult recommendation. The implemented rule is

```text
q_eff = max(q_adv,1-q_adv)
U = sign-adjusted S     if q_eff > p_I
U = I                   if q_eff <= p_I.
```

When `q_adv<1/2`, sign-adjusted `S` means inverting the adult recommendation. Exact equality goes to the private source rather than a random tie. With equal priors, independent binary recommendations, and known source reliabilities, this is a one-decision MAP-style rule. It is an oracle ceiling because a real juvenile is not given exact current `q_adv`, especially just after a shock. It is **not** a full sequential Bayesian learner: it has no posterior over source reliability, no explicit prior over environmental persistence, and no correction for dependence created when adult recommendations already embed earlier social information. `d` is inactive in Candidate C. Compare its accuracy, growth, recovery time, and selected `tau` against Candidate A; do not calculate a selection gradient in an inactive `d`.

This comparator is motivated by Bayesian cue-integration theory, which treats social and nonsocial information as inputs to one inference problem rather than as mutually exclusive learning modules.

## Candidate D — learned source reliability / reinforcement learning

Candidate D begins without access to true `q_adv`. The implementation gives every juvenile a finite trust score

```text
r in {-2,-1,0,+1,+2}
P(U=S | conflict,r) = logistic(trust_temperature * r).
```

After each encounter, objective full-information feedback is available with probability `feedback`. If it is available, trust moves one step up when the adult recommendation was correct and one step down when it was wrong, with reflecting caps. Trust does not require following the adult and does not estimate the private source, whose accuracy is fixed. With probability `1-feedback`, trust is unchanged. The public/score update still occurs. Newborns start at `r=0`; maturation carries both `Z` and `r`; adult private updating changes `Z` but not `r`.

This finite rule is related to, but deliberately simpler than, social-feature reward learning such as

```text
Q(a) = w_private x_private(a) + w_social x_social(a)
P(U=a) proportional to exp[Q(a)/temperature]
w' = w + alpha [reward-Q(U)] x(U).
```

Candidate D is path-dependent and feedback-dependent. It can be wrong after unlucky early outcomes, adapt after a shock, or fail when feedback is absent. Those possibilities are absent from the oracle Candidate C. Keep feedback probability, temperature, trust bounds, memory limits, and initial values fixed and nonheritable in v4; `d` is inactive. `(Z,r)` are cognitive/cultural substates inside `F` and `A`, not new demographic stages. Although adults retain `r`, current adult testimony and vital rates depend only on `Z`, so retained adult trust has no expressed adult effect.

Required Candidate D sensitivities:

- feedback probability from zero to one;
- the full-information assumption versus a future chosen-source-only extension;
- trust temperature and finite trust-bound sensitivity;
- reset versus retention of trust at maturation;
- stable versus shocked environments.

## Comparison matrix

| Criterion | A: fixed deference | B: private only | C: calibrated oracle | D: learned reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main role | Primary evolutionary model | Negative control | Upper-bound comparator | Plastic mechanistic comparator |
| Uses inherited `d` | Yes | No | No | No |
| Knows adult accuracy | No | N/A | Yes | Learns from feedback |
| Cultural advice endogenous | Yes | Ignored | Yes | Yes |
| Path-dependent source trust | Only via culture | No | No | Yes |
| Extra cognitive states | `Z` | `Z` | `Z` | `Z` plus learned values |
| Clean rare-mutant operator | Yes | Yes | Yes for `tau` | Yes if reliability is discretized; otherwise simulate |
| Main risk | Confounds action with internalization | Misses culture | Unrealistic oracle knowledge | Feedback/exploration assumptions dominate |

## How not to build rigidity into the answer

The model must not obtain conservatism by definition. Apply all of these constraints:

1. **No dogmatism genotype.** Do not inherit `H`, score leak, learning rate, trust updating, confidence threshold, adult updating rate, or post-shock reset speed.
2. **Fixed private accuracy.** Hold `p_I=0.65` when varying `d` and `tau`.
3. **No direct reward for deference.** Fitness responds to action/belief correctness only.
4. **No direct reward for delay.** `tau` changes time available to learn and time to reproduction, not fecundity by fiat.
5. **Do not equate confidence with correctness.** Track both conditional distributions.
6. **Expose the internalization assumption.** Treat public-only/no-internalization as the clean deference analysis.
7. **Allow correction.** Include fresh adult private updating and environmental shocks in declared sensitivities.
8. **Use one resident culture.** Never give each rare mutant a culture optimized for itself.
9. **Preserve unfavorable outcomes.** High `d` may be harmful, neutral, boundary-optimal, or unable to invade.
10. **Do not call a behavioral probability obedience, morality, or a personality trait.** It is a conflict-resolution rule in a hypothetical binary task.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Build and test the finite score kernels, including the analytical accuracy and `H=1` stationary benchmarks.
2. Implement the nonlinear monomorphic resident with density regulation and verify cultural retention through maturation.
3. Implement Candidate B and the public-only/no-internalization ablation.
4. Construct the frozen-resident rare-mutant operator and self-invasion tests.
5. Add shocks using saved common resident paths.
6. Add Candidate C as an oracle performance ceiling.
7. Add Candidate D only after feedback observability and update timing are explicit.
