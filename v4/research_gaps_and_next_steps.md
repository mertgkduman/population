## Measured post-experiment assessment

The executed experiments support cultural retention, conditional maturation benefits, slower shock recovery and a difference between invasion-selected maturation and abundance-maximizing maturation. They do not establish superiority over a general adaptive learner, permanent refusal to update, or multiple lasting cultural attractors. The strongest candidate contribution is the quantified shared-culture selection–abundance mismatch: tau≈.747 invades and replaces tau≈3.19 at d=1 while equilibrium abundance falls about 6%; low-density growth increases. The sign survives selected p and memory-cap perturbations, but novelty remains unestablished because cultural externalities and selection–abundance disagreement have prior theory. Read results.md for measured values and explicit limits. A rapid-switching follow-up supplies a counterexample to unconditional deference benefit. Reproduction and offline interaction checks pass, subject to the scope in verification.md.

The following literature-equivalence assessment was written before results and is retained as the pre-results record. References to “after results arrive” identify its original decision criteria.

## Literature assessment


The current V4 model does **not** introduce a clearly new elementary mechanism of confidence, cultural inertia, age-structured social learning, endogenous adult quality, or adult correction. Each ingredient has close primary-literature precedents. In particular, latent evidence or commitment that strengthens after repeated public observations appears in CODA and confident-voter models; age-structured cultural transmission and endogenous older-model quality appear in Fogarty et al. (2019) and Deffner and McElreath (2022); retained cultural memory appears in Ammar et al. (2023); social learning followed by individual learning appears in Lehmann et al. (2013) and related schedule models; and learned adviser reliability appears in Behrens et al. (2008) and Diaconescu et al. (2014).

The model may nevertheless support an **incremental theoretical contribution through its exact coupling**: a continuous-density two-stage demographic system over finite signed-confidence classes; juvenile conflict deference and maturation time as inherited traits; an anonymous adult testimony pool generated by the resident `A_z` distribution; score-preserving maturation; truth-directed adult correction; correctness-dependent vital rates; density regulation; shocks; and rare-mutant growth in the frozen resident ecological–cultural environment. I did not identify a single inspected primary model containing that entire combination. This is a bounded claim about the inspected literature, not proof of priority.

Paper-level contribution therefore depends on the results. A contribution is plausible if this coupling produces a robust, non-obvious evolutionary result that is not already implied by the one-decision identity `p + d(q-p)`, ordinary evidence accumulation, or the generic statement that memory slows reversal. Before those results exist, the appropriate status is:

> **Candidate incremental theoretical paper; component novelty is weak, combinatorial architecture is plausible, and result novelty is not yet established.**

## Terminology correction: this is not a standard bounded-confidence model

The score is bounded, but the social interaction rule is not “bounded confidence” in the standard opinion-dynamics sense. In the Deffuant model, two agents influence one another only when their continuous opinions differ by less than a threshold; in Hegselmann–Krause, each agent averages over the opinions inside an epsilon-neighborhood. The confidence bound selects **which other opinions are admitted**. See [Deffuant et al. (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219525900000078) and [Hegselmann and Krause (2002)](https://www.jasss.org/5/3/2.html).

V4 instead admits every sampled adult recommendation and moves a signed score by one step, with reflecting caps. The code describes the discrete values as `H:int=6`, `p:float=.65`, `adult_rate:float=.3`, `internalize:bool=True`, and `reset_maturation:bool=False` [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:9-33`]. The cap implementation is explicitly `"Reflecting score caps: a contradictory update always moves inward."` [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:51-61`]. There is no distance-dependent exclusion of an interlocutor.

Use one of these descriptions:

- **finite-cap signed evidence accumulator**;
- **bounded endorsement-score random walk**;
- **finite-confidence cultural-state model**;
- **confidence-induced cultural persistence**, when discussing the consequence rather than the state variable.

Avoid “a bounded-confidence model” unless the text immediately states that “bounded” refers to the finite score range, not to Deffuant/Hegselmann–Krause confidence-set interaction.

## What the implementation actually adds

The model state is a density over two demographic stages, signed score, and—only in the RL policy—a five-level trust state. The source-of-truth values are `shape(c): return (2*c.H+1,5 if c.policy=='rl' else 1)` and the accepted policies are exactly `('fixed','private','oracle','rl')` [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:36-44`]. This matters because novelty must be assessed against the implemented mechanism, not against a richer verbal interpretation.

### Endogenous adult testimony

Adult advice quality is calculated from the current adult distribution's **sign accuracy**, with a tie at `z=0` contributing one half, followed by a symmetric transmission error [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:64-69`]. This makes testimony culturally endogenous rather than an imposed time series. However:

- the recommending adult is anonymous;
- the adult's `|z|` is not transmitted;
- a high-confidence and a low-confidence adult with the same sign emit the same recommendation distribution;
- no prestige, teaching effort, strategic intent, kin relation, genealogy, or network position is represented;
- the learner receives only the pool-level marginal `q`.

Thus “endogenous adult testimony” is correct. “Confidence-weighted testimony,” “prestige learning,” “parental teaching,” and “lineage-aware social evidence” are not.

### Retained score at maturation

With the default `reset_maturation:bool=False`, maturation transfers juvenile density from `F_z` to `A_z` without changing `z`; reset is a declared ablation [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:27-29,94-105`]. This is a clear and useful bridge from development to adult cultural supply. It is not, by itself, a new memory mechanism: life-history learning schedules, embodied knowledge retained into reproductive life, and cultural repertoires with memory already exist in primary theory.

### Adult private correction

Adults receive a fixed-rate, fixed-accuracy private update that shifts the same score toward or away from the current true state [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:120-121`]. This creates reopening after maturation and a truth-directed route out of stale culture. It is not evidence-contingent metacognition, a Bayesian posterior reset, or adult learning of source reliability. Later individual learning following earlier social learning is already a central schedule in [Lehmann et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/evo.12040); Deffner and McElreath also allow adult learning in their individual-based sensitivity model ([open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9012401/)).

### Uncritical internalization

In the fixed policy, the chosen public action determines the score update when `internalize=True`; with `internalize=False`, the private signal determines the score update while deference can still affect public action and juvenile mortality [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:94-109`]. This is scientifically important because inherited `d` then packages together:

1. action choice on private/adult conflict; and
2. which action is written into persistent cultural memory.

The main model therefore estimates a **deference–internalization package**, not pure behavioral deference. The public-only ablation is required for the narrower estimand. Closely related action-to-latent-belief reinforcement already exists in [Martins' CODA model](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129183108012339), although the demographic and truth-linked components differ.

### Rare-mutant analysis

The generator freezes resident `q` and the resident resource factor when evaluating a rare mutant [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:142-153`]. This properly separates resident construction of the cultural environment from mutant growth at rarity. It is a strong methodological feature of the project. It should be presented as an application of invasion analysis to this coupled cultural-demographic state system, not as a new cultural mechanism.

### Equivalence judgment

No single inspected model is an exact mathematical duplicate of V4. The closest **cognitive kernel** is CODA plus a finite cap and exogenous truth-linked correction. The closest **confidence/inertia kernel** is confident or persistent voter dynamics. The closest **cultural-demographic architecture** is the family containing Fogarty et al. (2019), Deffner and McElreath (2022), Lehmann et al. (2013), and Maisonneuve et al. (2025). The closest **source-learning comparator** is the Behrens/Diaconescu adviser-learning family.

Accordingly, the likely contribution is architectural and evolutionary, not the discovery of evidence accumulation, cultural inertia, developmental schedules, or trust learning.

### Ledger conclusion

All thirteen records have useful source-backed mechanisms. The largest overreach risk lies in treating their V4-facing implications as findings of the cited paper. In particular:

- Simon does not validate the implemented accuracy rule as a model of altruism.

## Explicit unresolved gaps, ranked

### 1. Establish a result that is not an algebraic or generic-memory consequence

This is the immediate paper gate. The exact one-decision accuracy is already preregistered as

```text
P(public action correct) = p + d(q_adv-p).
```

The source-of-truth question file quotes the corresponding values `Private accuracy: fixed at p=0.65` and `Inherited traits: juvenile conflict deference d and mean maturation time tau only` [VERIFIED: `v4/research_questions.md:3-6`]. Therefore:

- a positive `d` effect when `q>p` is an identity, not a discovery;
- a sign reversal in the instantaneous `d` gradient when `q` crosses `p` is an identity, not a discovery;
- stronger score persistence at larger `H` and slower response to opposing evidence are expected accumulator behavior;
- “stable environments favor social information more than rapidly changing environments” is established prior theory.

Potentially paper-level results would instead include a robust reversal between isolated growth and resident-conditioned invasion; a nontrivial `d`–`tau` selection surface caused by ecological–cultural feedback rather than the frozen kernel; multiple resident cultural attractors that change mutual invasibility; or a reproducible non-monotone shock/memory threshold that survives internalization, reset, adult-update, payoff, and horizon checks. The causal decomposition must show which term creates the result.

**Required next step:** after results arrive, classify every main finding as (a) exact identity, (b) known qualitative effect, (c) new consequence of the V4 coupling, or (d) numerical artifact/parameter-specific observation. Only category (c) can anchor novelty.

### 2. Source lineage and correlation are not explicitly modeled

V4's adult pool is culturally endogenous, but each encounter is summarized by the current marginal `q`. It does not retain who learned from whom, whether two adults share the same original evidence, whether a juvenile repeatedly samples the same adult, or which public actions are copied from earlier public actions. This distinction is essential:

- **Population feedback is present:** past social learning changes the adult distribution that generates later advice.
- **Source-dependency inference is absent:** a learner cannot discount redundant advice because identity, genealogy, and communication pathways do not exist in the state.

[Whalen et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12485) show why independent and shared-source testimony should carry different evidential weight. [Fränken et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2023.105633) compare dependency-sensitive and naïve aggregation in communication networks. These are closer precedents for the missing mechanism than simply citing cascades.

**Claim boundary now:** say “the adult marginal is endogenous and can recycle culture.” Do not say “the learner models cascades,” “discounts correlated sources,” or “tracks cultural lineage.”

**Future extension:** add persistent adviser or lineage identifiers, a network/parentage structure, and a learner state that distinguishes first-hand from copied evidence. This should be a separate extension after the minimal model is understood, because it changes the state space and the meaning of `q`.

### 3. The oracle is a one-decision source selector, not a full Bayesian learner

The implemented oracle follows the adult source when `max(q,1-q)>p` and inverts an anti-reliable adult source through `max(q,1-q)` [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:71-80,94-97`]. Under equal priors, known source reliabilities, and a single pair of conditionally independent binary recommendations, this is a reasonable MAP-style decision rule: agree when the cues agree, and on disagreement choose the more reliable (possibly inverted) cue.

It is not “true Bayes” across development because it has:

- exact contemporaneous knowledge of `q` rather than a posterior over adult reliability;
- no uncertainty about `p` or `q`;
- no posterior over environmental state or volatility;
- no sequential accumulation of likelihood ratios from both sources;
- no causal model of source dependence or cultural lineage;
- no adviser identity or intention;
- no value-of-information or exploration decision.

[Perreault et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2011.12.007) is the appropriate cultural-evolution anchor for treating social and nonsocial cues as one inferential problem. [Diaconescu et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003810) is a stricter adviser-learning comparison because it represents beliefs about advice validity and volatility and integrates social with nonsocial information.

**Claim boundary now:** “oracle-calibrated one-decision information ceiling” is accurate. “Bayesian learner,” “Bayes-optimal developmental policy,” or “normative upper bound under correlated cultural evidence” is not.

**Future comparator:** implement a sequential posterior over environment and adult reliability, ideally with a dependence parameter. Do this only if the fixed/oracle/RL results make inference quality central to the paper; otherwise state the limitation rather than expanding scope.

### 4. The RL model has a joint density state, but not joint individual inference or control

The ODE does preserve a joint density over `(z,r)` in each stage. Trust has exactly five states because the code uses `np.arange(-2,3)`, and follow probability is a fixed logistic function of that trust state [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:44,71-75`]. Under feedback, `r` rises or falls with actual adult correctness, while `z` stores the selected action (or the private signal in the ablation) [VERIFIED: `v4/experiments/model.py:110-121`].

What is missing is an individual RL problem with joint value or belief state. There is no learned value function or `Q(z,r,age,context,action)`, no credit assignment to chosen versus unchosen sources, no learned private-source reliability, no adviser identity, no volatility state, and no exploration policy. Feedback is full information: the model can update adult trust based on adult correctness even when the adult was not followed. Retained adult `r` also has no expressed adult effect because adult testimony and vital rates depend on `z`, not `r`.

This makes Candidate D a finite **reliability counter**, not a general RL agent. Behrens et al. (2008) already separate reward and confederate prediction errors and adjust learning to source volatility; Diaconescu et al. (2014) compare hierarchical inference with simpler learning models.

**Required reporting now:** call it “finite feedback-based trust learning with full-information outcomes.” Report `feedback=0` and `feedback=1`, trust retention/reset, and the fact that `d` is inactive. Do not call it a psychologically matched competitor.

**Future extension:** chosen-source-only and delayed feedback, learned `p`, adviser-specific trust, and a joint partially observed state are the clearest upgrades. They should not be added merely to improve performance; each changes the inferential question.

### 5. Cooperation, strategic teaching, and parent–offspring conflict are not tested

The model has no cost of giving testimony, no adult choice about whether or what to transmit, no parent identifier, no relatedness, no inclusive-fitness effect, no strategic deception, no prestige benefit, and no mismatch between parent and offspring genetic interests. Adult testimony is an anonymous, costless draw from the adult cultural distribution. Consequently, the experiments cannot establish:

- evolution of cooperation or altruism;
- Simonian socially induced altruistic behavior;
- prestige as freely conferred deference;
- teaching as a costly adaptation;
- parent–offspring conflict over learning or maturation;
- vertical versus oblique conflict;
- manipulation by adults or resistance by juveniles.

[Maisonneuve et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2024.2470) explicitly models costly parental teaching and kin-selection feedback, making it a direct warning against importing “teaching” into V4's pooled testimony. If parent–offspring conflict becomes a target, the model must distinguish genetic parent, cultural model, and population adult pool and must assign actor and recipient payoffs separately.

**Recommendation:** keep this outside the present paper unless the central V4 result specifically creates a compelling conflict prediction. Adding it now would turn a minimal deference/inertia study into a different model.

## Negative-evidence and interpretation checklist

The following are meaningful absences from the implementation, not claims that the entire literature lacks them:

| Not represented in V4 | Consequence for interpretation |
|---|---|
| Adviser identity or repeated teacher relationship | No selective trust in particular adults and no reputation |
| Genealogy, parentage, or communication network | No explicit source-lineage correlation, vertical transmission, or network cascade |
| Transmission of `|z|` | Adult confidence magnitude affects persistence and fitness but not testimony strength |
| Strategic testimony or teaching cost | No cooperation, deception, persuasion investment, or teaching evolution |
| Separate persistent private belief and public expression in the main model | Under internalization, public choice is written into the score; Gavrilets-style attitude/action lag is not implemented |
| Second-order beliefs | No pluralistic-ignorance mechanism |
| Posterior uncertainty or volatility state | Oracle and RL cannot represent Bayesian confidence in source reliability |
| Learned private-source reliability | `p` is fixed and known to the comparator design |
| Adult expression of retained RL trust | Adult `r` is carried but behaviorally inert in adulthood |
| Genetic `H`, adult learning rate, trust memory, or rigidity | Selection conclusions apply only to inherited `d` and `tau` |
| Empirical calibration to a species or population | Results are theoretical and dimensionless, not estimates of human effects |

The model is a deterministic density system that exactly averages its specified Bernoulli events, not an individual-based sample path. This is a strength for clean population dynamics, but finite-population drift, individual network histories, and within-agent trajectory correlations outside `(z,r)` are not estimated.

1. **Strong theoretical paper:** a robust resident-conditioned invasion, attractor, or non-monotone timescale result survives all preregistered ablations and has a causal decomposition not reducible to the frozen action-accuracy identity.
2. **Useful focused methods/model note:** the exact state system and invasion machinery clarify why monomorphic performance and invasion differ, even if qualitative learning results are familiar.
3. **Negative or boundary result:** deference is neutral or selected out once adult correction/internalization is controlled, or all rankings collapse to the obvious `q-p` rule. This can still be valuable if presented as a sharp constraint on stronger docility or cultural-inertia narratives.
4. **Insufficient standalone contribution:** the principal findings are only that better information improves accuracy, larger memory slows reversal, or social learning fares worse after rapid change, without a distinct evolutionary consequence.

## Measured age-hazard robustness result

The separate `experiments/age_robustness.py` check replaces the single memoryless juvenile compartment with four serial juvenile phases. Progression hazard is `4/tau`, so mean maturation time remains `tau` while its coefficient of variation falls from `1` to `1/2`. All juvenile phases retain the same cultural score and sum to `F`; the last progression enters `A` at the same score. The comparison uses `d in {0,.5,1}`, `tau in {1,3,8}`, stable and one-flip-at-150 environments, `T=400`, `dt=.05`, `H=6`, and `p=.65`.

The `L=1` implementation matches the core trajectory exactly: maximum saved-state and reported-metric absolute error is `0` in both stable and shock scenarios. Relative to memoryless maturation, `L=4` changes total-horizon growth `rT=log[N(T)/N(0)]/T` by only `-4.90e-4` to `+4.18e-4` across the tested grid, but raises final adult accuracy by `0.00788` to `0.06852` and raises the adult high-absolute-score fraction by `0.00131` to `0.23023`. The largest confidence-state differences occur at `tau=8`. Under the shock, the narrower age distribution can either accelerate or slow recovery; the most persistent cell (`d=1,tau=8`) recovers to the declared accuracy threshold at time `47` after the flip versus `31` for `L=1`, and its integrated accuracy debt is `19.99` versus `10.16`.

Interpretation: replacing exponential maturation with a less variable equal-mean Erlang hazard does not simply rescale `tau`. It changes the distribution of juvenile exposure histories, increasing score depth and eventual adult accuracy while sometimes increasing post-shock inertia. The sign of its effect on long-horizon population growth depends on `d` and `tau`. This is a robustness qualification, not a new mechanism or evidence that `L=4` is biologically correct. It strengthens the case for reporting the maturation-time distribution, rather than describing `tau` as a fixed birthday.

Machine-readable results are in `experiments/age_robustness.json` and `experiments/age_robustness.csv`.
