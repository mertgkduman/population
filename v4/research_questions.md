# v4 research questions and preregistered hypotheses

**Frozen:** 2026-10-30, before any broad v4 parameter sweep  
**Scope:** hypothetical two-stage gene–culture model; not fitted to humans or any named species  
**Private accuracy:** fixed at `p=0.65` in every primary comparison  
**Inherited traits:** juvenile conflict deference `d` and mean maturation time `tau` only

## Primary question

When juveniles receive a fresh fixed-accuracy private recommendation and a recommendation sampled from an endogenous adult cultural distribution, under what conditions can an inherited probability of choosing the adult recommendation on conflict invade, persist, or be selected against, and how does that selection interact with the time available before maturation?

The answer must separate:

1. immediate public-action accuracy and juvenile survival;
2. acquired score, stored belief, and confidence;
3. cultural carryover when juveniles mature without a score reset;
4. resident ecological–cultural equilibrium outcomes;
5. rare-mutant growth in the same resident culture and resource environment;
6. stable-environment performance from adaptation after shocks.

## Secondary questions

1. Does deference have an effect beyond private-only learning when adult testimony is generated by adults who learned as juveniles?
2. Is any advantage of `d` an immediate behavioral substitution effect, or does it require writing the endorsed action into stored score?
3. Can a longer mean juvenile period improve adult cultural accuracy enough to offset delayed reproduction and juvenile mortality without an exogenous `tau` reward?
4. Does bounded score depth create a retention–reversal trade-off after environmental change?
5. Does fixed inherited deference remain competitive with an oracle-calibrated one-decision selector and a finite feedback-based trust learner?
6. Are apparent resident optima also locally uninvadable under the frozen-resident rare-mutant operator?
7. Are conclusions conditional on one resident cultural attractor or robust to initially correct, neutral, and wrong adult cultures?

## Literature-derived hypotheses

These are translations of prior theory into the v4 mechanisms, not claims that v4 is a reproduction of the cited models.

### L1 — social receptivity has an accuracy threshold

**Source:** Simon's docility argument requires the gross fitness benefit of receptivity to exceed its associated cost; the v4 binary decision rule supplies an exact narrow version of that premise.

**Prediction:** In the fixed-policy model at a frozen resident adult accuracy `q_adv`, the immediate effect of higher `d` has the sign of `q_adv-p`:

```text
P(public action correct) = p + d(q_adv-p).
```

Higher `d` lowers juvenile error mortality when `q_adv>p`, raises it when `q_adv<p`, and is neutral when `q_adv=p`.

**Decisive test:** Freeze culture and resource at matched values, vary `d`, and compare the numerical juvenile transition/death rates with the formula.

**Disconfirmation:** A nonzero `d` effect at `q_adv=p` in the implemented fixed mean-field model, or a sign opposite to `q_adv-p`, indicates a coding or bookkeeping error. This null does not apply if another explicit `d` cost or source-dependent state is later introduced.

### L2 — environmental change weakens inherited social information and can make learning time non-monotone

**Source:** Lehmann, Wakano & Aoki (2013) found that oblique social learning can precede individual learning when retained information is useful, while increasing environmental change first shifts effort toward compensatory individual learning and, at sufficiently high change, can eliminate long learning periods.

**Prediction:** As the environmental shock rate rises, the long-run advantage of fixed high `d` declines because adult culture is stale for a larger fraction of time. The selected or growth-maximizing `tau` need not decrease monotonically: moderate change may favor more time for correction, whereas high change should make long retention unprofitable.

**Decisive test:** Estimate paired common-shock Lyapunov growth over a predeclared shock-rate grid and inspect `d` and `tau` reaction norms. Report a monotone boundary result if no hump appears.

**Disconfirmation:** No systematic change is also informative if adult private updating makes culture track shocks faster than the tested shock scale. The hypothesis is rejected, not rescued by retuning rates after the sweep.

### L3 — self-reinforcing cultural conservatism requires a transmission rule that can protect itself

**Source:** Acerbi, Enquist & Ghirlanda (2009) showed that culturally transmitted preferences affecting copying can become self-protecting, while prolonged acquisition of traits needed for model effectiveness can preserve openness.

**Prediction:** Uncritical internalization will produce more score persistence and stronger culture-mediated effects of `d` than the public-only ablation, because choosing a recommendation also writes it into the future adult score. The public-only model should retain the current-action effect but substantially reduce the intergenerational feedback.

**Decisive test:** At identical traits, demography, private accuracy, adult updating, and shock sequence, compare internalization on versus off for adult belief accuracy, high-absolute-score mass, recovery time, and mutant growth.

**Disconfirmation:** If the two mechanisms are indistinguishable, internalization is not carrying the result in that region. If internalization alone creates the claimed effect, describe it as a deference–internalization package, not emergent genetic dogmatism.

### L4 — unpredictable social value favors experience-dependent flexibility

**Source:** van den Berg, Vu & Molleman (2024) found that developmental flexibility in social reliance evolves when the benefit of social information is unpredictable, whereas predictably high or low social value produces inflexible high or low reliance. Schultner, Molleman & Lindstrom (2025) provide a reward-learning mechanism for experience-dependent social weights.

**Prediction:** Candidate D's finite trust learner will outperform any single fixed `d` in regimes where adult accuracy varies across time and objective feedback is frequent enough to track it. Fixed extreme strategies can match or beat D when adult value is consistently high or consistently low, or when feedback is too sparse.

**Decisive test:** Compare long-run public accuracy, demographic growth, and post-shock accuracy debt under identical adult culture paths for fixed `d`, oracle, and RL policies. Include `feedback=0` and `feedback=1`.

**Disconfirmation:** If RL dominates without feedback, its implementation is leaking current `q_adv` or truth. If it never adapts with full feedback, inspect trust transitions before changing the hypothesis.

### L5 — retained social knowledge can support longer development only through an endogenous information benefit

**Source:** Lehmann et al. (2013) and Maisonneuve, Lehmann & Mullon (2025) couple learning schedules to knowledge retained across generations; the latter shows that intergenerational social transmission and teaching can feed back on cumulative knowledge.

**Prediction:** With low shock/obsolescence, state-preserving maturation and adult-to-juvenile testimony can generate an interior or extended `tau` region because additional juvenile encounters improve the adult score distribution and hence adult survival/productivity. Maturation reset, no social channel, or no correctness payoff should remove that route.

**Decisive test:** Compare the primary model with `reset_maturation=True`, private-only policy, and `payoff=False`, while retaining the same maturation hazard and initial conditions.

**Disconfirmation:** If the same `tau` benefit remains with both retained culture and correctness payoffs removed, it comes from another demographic mechanism or a bug. A lower-bound optimum in the primary model is a valid rejection.

## Original testable hypotheses

“Original” here means proposed for this v4 experiment, not a literature-wide novelty claim.

### O1 — deference changes sign during recovery from a shock

**Prediction:** Immediately after a sign flip, resident adult advice will be worse than the fixed private source, so higher `d` will be harmful. After adult updating and cohort replacement raise `q_adv` through `p`, the instantaneous selection gradient on `d` will reverse. The integrated effect over one shock interval will change sign at a finite relationship between shock frequency and cultural recovery time.

**Metrics:** time series of `q_adv-p`, public accuracy, paired mutant log-ratio, time of `q_adv=p`, and cumulative accuracy debt before and after that crossing.

**Falsifier:** No sign reversal despite a verified crossing of `q_adv=p` in the fixed frozen-path mutant calculation.

### O2 — `d` and `tau` are complementary only when adult advice is better than private information

**Prediction:** Under internalization, the drift of the juvenile score toward the correct sign is

```text
2[p+d(q_adv-p)]-1.
```

When `q_adv>p`, extra juvenile encounters from a longer `tau` amplify the benefit of `d`; when `q_adv<p`, they amplify its cultural cost. Therefore the local `d`–`tau` interaction should change sign with `q_adv-p`, before accounting for survival selection and reproductive delay.

**Metrics:** finite differences of frozen-culture invasion fitness and score-at-maturation accuracy across matched `d,tau` cells.

**Falsifier:** A robust opposite interaction after the learning-only kernel has passed its exact benchmark; if demography reverses the learning interaction, report the decomposition rather than rejecting the kernel prediction.

### O3 — deeper bounded confidence trades stable performance for slower reversal

**Prediction:** Increasing `H` will improve retention or reduce boundary noise in a stable environment but increase post-shock wrong-sign persistence and recovery time. Consequently, long-run performance under recurrent shocks can be non-monotone in `H`, even though `H` is fixed and nonheritable.

**Metrics:** stable adult belief accuracy, mean `|Z|`, wrong-sign mass, high-confidence wrong mass, chance-crossing time, half-recovery time, and Lyapunov growth for `H in {1,3,6,9}`.

**Falsifier:** Recovery is invariant to `H` after solver and horizon checks, or the apparent relationship is entirely an initialization artifact.

### O4 — plastic source trust flattens the value of inherited fixed deference

**Prediction:** The oracle and RL comparators will reduce the attainable advantage of the best fixed `d` by changing source use within a lifetime. RL will approach the oracle as feedback becomes frequent and the environment changes slowly relative to trust updating, but will lag or overshoot after abrupt shocks.

**Metrics:** regret relative to the oracle, public accuracy, accuracy debt, population growth, and selected `tau`. `d` is inactive and must not be shown as an evolved axis for oracle or RL.

**Falsifier:** RL matches the oracle immediately after a shock from neutral trust without enough feedback; this would indicate an information leak.

### O5 — resident culture can reverse rankings from isolated demographic growth

**Prediction:** A trait pair that grows fastest when evaluated in its own culture or at low density need not invade a resident that supplies a different adult advice distribution and resource level. Pairwise invasibility rankings will differ from at least some isolated-population rankings when cultural construction is strong.

**Metrics:** isolated low-density growth, monomorphic equilibrium abundance/culture, `s(g_M;g_R)` from the frozen-resident Metzler operator, and mutual-invasion classification.

**Falsifier:** Identical rankings across all tested conditions. That result is legitimate and should not be forced to differ.

## Predeclared nulls and ablations

| ID | Manipulation | Expected diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | `q_adv=p` in frozen fixed-policy model | Full generator and mortality are invariant to `d` |
| N2 | `payoff=False` | All correctness-dependent survival/productivity selection disappears; cultural states may still differ |
| N3 | Private policy / `d=0` | Current public accuracy equals `p`; adult advice cannot affect juvenile choice |
| N4 | `internalize=False` | `d` changes public action and juvenile mortality but stored juvenile score follows the private source |
| N5 | `reset_maturation=True` | Maturation sends all flux to adult `z=0` and trust-neutral state; retained juvenile culture is removed |
| N6 | `adult_rate=0` | Adult beliefs change only through cohort replacement and differential vital rates |
| N7 | `H=1` | Immediate source choice remains but deep score persistence is removed |
| N8 | `p=0.5` diagnostic only | No systematic truth-directed recovery without another truth-linked channel |
| N9 | Correct, neutral, and wrong initial adult cultures | Reveals multiple resident attractors or initialization dependence |
| N10 | Resident self-mutant | Both `||L_R x_R*||/||x_R*||` and spectral abscissa are approximately zero |

## Experimental sequence

The order is fixed to prevent a favorable broad sweep from determining the hypotheses.

### Stage 1 — algebra and event-kernel checks

- enumerate `H=1` transitions at both environmental signs;
- check reflecting caps move inward under contradictory updates;
- verify learning transitions conserve mass;
- verify births enter `F_(z=0,r=0)`;
- verify state-preserving `tau^-1 F_z,r -> A_z,r` when reset is off;
- verify the exact action-accuracy formula and equal-accuracy null;
- verify oracle inversion for `q_adv<0.5`;
- verify RL trust moves according to realized adult correctness, not expected `q_adv`.

### Stage 2 — resident pilot

Run a small factorial pilot around the implemented defaults:

```text
H=6, p=.65, juvenile_rate=1, adult_rate=.3,
transmission_error=.05, d in {0,.5,1}, tau in {1,4,12}.
```

Start from correct, neutral, and wrong adult cultures. This pilot selects numerical horizon and tolerances only; it must not be used to rewrite directional hypotheses.

### Stage 3 — static resident and invasion sweep

Primary trait grid:

```text
d:   21 evenly spaced values on [0,1]
tau: 25 logarithmically spaced values on [0.5,20]
```

Refine around candidate singularities or boundary transitions with a 51-by-51 local grid. For every resident used in invasion analysis, save equilibrium residual, adult culture, resource factor, operator spectral abscissa, and attractor initialization.

### Stage 4 — shock experiments

Use paired common environment paths. Express shock rates relative to `juvenile_rate=1`; include stable, slow, intermediate, and fast regimes chosen before looking at trait outcomes. Pre-equilibrate residents before shocks. Report censored recovery thresholds rather than replacing non-recovery with the horizon.

### Stage 5 — mechanism and comparator experiments

Run the full internalization, reset, adult-update, `H`, and payoff ablations. Then compare private, oracle, and RL policies on identical resident/shock paths. The oracle is an information ceiling, not a psychologically matched learner. RL uses full-information Bernoulli feedback in the implemented model; chosen-source-only or delayed feedback remains an untested extension unless separately implemented.

## Required outputs

### State and culture

- complete `F_z` and `A_z` distributions, and `F_z,r`, `A_z,r` for RL;
- stored belief accuracy in juveniles and adults;
- public action accuracy;
- adult testimony accuracy before transmission and `q_adv` after transmission error;
- mean `|Z|` and mean signed score relative to the environment;
- wrong-sign and high-absolute-score wrong-sign mass;
- maturation-flux score distribution, not only standing juveniles/adults;
- RL trust distribution and adult-follow probability.

### Demography and evolution

- births, juvenile deaths, adult deaths, maturation flux, density, and per-capita growth;
- isolated low-density growth, clearly labeled;
- resident equilibrium abundance and culture;
- frozen-resident rare-mutant spectral abscissa;
- mutual-invasion outcome and local selection gradients;
- Floquet or pathwise Lyapunov exponent under changing environments.

### Recovery

- chance-crossing time;
- half-recovery time;
- integrated accuracy debt;
- right-censor indicator;
- paired clone-normalized mutant growth.

## Decision rules and claim discipline

1. Do not select a preferred parameter set because it produces an interior optimum or positive `d` effect.
2. Preserve flat, negative, boundary, bistable, and non-recovery results.
3. A low-density growth optimum is not an ESS or invasion result.
4. A positive rare-mutant exponent establishes local invasion in a specified resident attractor, not fixation.
5. “Confidence-induced cultural persistence” is the primary term. The code's `dogmatism` field is only the fraction of adults with `|Z|>3`, a persistence-capacity proxy; it is not a psychological diagnosis.
6. Keep the corrected outputs separate: `private_sampling=nu_F` counts private-signal draws at every juvenile encounter, whereas `private_choice_on_conflict=1-E_F(w_r)` is the conditional propensity to choose the private recommendation on disagreement.
7. Candidate C has contemporaneous oracle knowledge and Candidate D has full-information outcome feedback; neither is an equally informed human cognitive model.
8. Candidate A with internalization estimates deference plus memory assimilation. The public-only ablation is required for the narrow behavioral estimand.
9. No result establishes genetic separability of deference from cognition, the historical evolution of childhood, obedience, morality, norms, or altruism.
