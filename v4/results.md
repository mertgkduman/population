# V4 results: endogenous culture, development, and conditional selection

All results are hypothetical simulation data. Primary private signal accuracy is p=.65 for every genotype and policy. Arbitrary units are used. Source `experiments/findings.json` is regenerated by `analyze_results.py`; individual runs, population distributions and fluxes are retained in NPZ files. No empirical fit or biological heritability claim is made.

## What was actually simulated

The preregistered sequence ran 196 pilot trajectories, 5,130 distinct main trajectories (9,690 displayed grid cells because inactive comparator d rows repeat), 2,442 refinement trajectories, 1,080 stochastic-environment records across 40 histories, 75 memory/feedback sensitivities, 36 additional ablations, age-hazard checks, 242 pairwise invasibility cells, 99 dynamic mutant rates from 11 resident histories, explicit mutation-selection competition, a maturation selection-gradient refinement, and independent numerical verification. A final volatility follow-up adds 24 histories and 216 trait trajectories, summarized as 72 paired seed-by-tau rows. Additional follow-ups examine next-generation reproduction, out-of-sample parameters, and explicit rare-lineage replacement. Repeated displayed comparator cells are not independent experiments.

The core is a deterministic distribution of signed confidence and, for RL, individual learned trust. It is not an individual-based animal simulation. Private/testimony events are averaged exactly by the transition generator; randomness in replicated experiments is the externally generated environmental history. Newborns have neutral scores, not inherited factual content. Each adult retains its juvenile state at maturation and continues to receive private evidence. Adult advice accuracy is generated by the adult state distribution.

## Established consequences reproduced

For a fixed culture of advice accuracy q, public action accuracy is p+d(q-p). Equal q=p is a true d-neutral control in this particular mean-field generator. No-payoff controls remove d's demographic effect; tau still matters because maturation delays reproduction. No direct d reward, direct tau productivity curve, or inherited adult rigidity parameter is present.

The oracle benchmark reads true source accuracy, including after a shock, and can invert an anti-informative source. It is a one-decision Bayesian bound under the stated symmetric signal assumptions, not a sequential Bayesian cultural learner with realistic uncertainty. The finite trust learner receives individual Bernoulli outcome feedback, has a common five-level trust score and softmax policy, and resets trust at birth. It is one reinforcement-learning implementation, not a representative of all possible learning algorithms.

## Endogenous knowledge and a conditional preparation benefit

At the grid tau nearest 3 (2.9225), increasing fixed d from 0 to 1 raises stationary adult belief accuracy from .78369 to .86158 and N(400) from 4218.8 to 5823.6. The fraction of adult scores that would retain their sign after three verified opposing unit updates rises from .21289 to .31848. This is **confidence-score persistence**, not proof of dogmatic refusal to update.

In the public-only ablation, the action may follow adults but stored confidence uses private evidence. The corresponding d=1 adult accuracy is .78672, persistence .22004, and N(400)=4640.3. Thus immediate behavioral substitution accounts for some benefit; storing selected testimony creates an additional cultural feedback. Persistence is therefore conditional on an explicitly stated internalization rule. It is not generated without assumptions about memory.

The refined stable fixed-policy maximum of isolated r400/N400 occurs at d=1 and tau=3.1922, interior in tau but at the upper d boundary. Public-only and memoryless alternatives instead maximize at the lower tau grid boundary .5. Longer preparation can matter through acquired adult knowledge in the selected model, without an imposed fecundity-vs-tau curve. This does not establish that the same tau is selected within a mixed population.

## Environmental change and correction

At tau=2.9225, fixed d=0 reaches half of its own stable adult accuracy advantage over chance 10 model-time units after the shock; fixed d=1 takes 20. Resolution is 5 time units, with success required over the next 20 units. Integrated adult accuracy deficit relative to its own no-shock trajectory rises from 4.184 to 10.476 accuracy×time units. Public-only d=1 takes 10 units and has deficit 4.502; RL takes 15 and has deficit 7.091.

These measurements avoid a common error discovered by adversarial review: some populations never reach a universal .75 accuracy threshold even after fully recovering to their baseline. The original absolute-.75 metric is retained separately as `recovery_absolute75`; the primary `recovery` now uses the matched baseline. Missing recovery is right-censoring, not zero.

Adults always admit contradictory evidence under the primary update rule. There are no absorbing confidence boundaries. An initially wrong culture converges toward the same stable regime under tested informative-private-evidence conditions; the tested initializations do not establish bistability. Uninformative private signals alone do not remove all access to truth: correctness-dependent mortality/productivity still filters culture. When both private informativeness and that payoff channel are removed, adult accuracy approaches chance, as the combined negative control shows.

## Evolution differs from isolated abundance

Rare mutants hear the resident adults and experience the resident resource level. They do not receive a separate culture of mutant teachers. Mutation-selection competition transmits d and tau through births, with culture reset at birth and learned subsequently. The broad competition grid uses mutation .002 to any candidate class; it demonstrates trait-frequency change, not a proven ESS.

A coarse stable competition initially suggested modal tau=.5. A nearby tau=.55 had positive invasion, so that coarse endpoint was rejected as a stable optimum. Refining the invasion gradient at d=1 gives a candidate tau=.74655. The gradient points toward it from either side; none of 1,281 sampled mutants in d∈[0,1], tau∈[.2,12] had positive invasion at that resident. This is a numerically supported candidate within tested ranges, not an analytical ESS proof. Deference remains at its upper boundary.

At the isolated-abundance optimum tau=3.1922, a rare tau=.74655 lineage has invasion rate +.0010006 per time and next-generation reproduction R0=1.01026. Its eventual monoculture equilibrium abundance is 5476.2 versus 5828.0 for the original resident. This mismatch persists at p=.60,.75 and memory caps H=3,9 with all other parameters matched. The explanation is a familiar cultural/ecological externality: a mutant takes advantage of shared adult information while altering its own delay to reproduction and future contribution to that culture.

Crucially, this is **lower equilibrium abundance**, not evidence that selection reduced low-density population growth. Independently normalized low-density calculations give rates .21750 for tau=.74655 versus .14951 for tau=3.1922. All positive stationary populations have zero realized net growth at equilibrium. The next-generation R0 signs independently agree with the dominant-eigenvalue invasion signs. The explicit zero-mutation two-lineage run starts the shorter-maturation lineage at 0.1%. By time 12,000 its frequency is effectively one (the original resident frequency is 1.66×10^-21), and abundance falls to 5476.2. This replacement was simulated with shared teachers and resource, rather than inferred from invasion alone. The original resident frequency is a deterministic density ratio, not literal stochastic extinction.

## Replication and uncertainty

Forty telegraph-environment histories were split beforehand into 20 exploratory and 20 confirmatory seeds. Within each history, all compared traits share exactly the same environmental changes. For fixed d=1 minus d=0 at tau=3, mean r600 differences are +.000177 (exploratory) and +.000253 (confirmatory). The confirmatory 95% interval for the paired mean is approximately [.000070,.000435]; only 15 of 20 individual confirmatory histories have positive differences. At tau=8 the interval includes zero. Thus there is no claim that deference helps in every changing environment.

These intervals describe sampling variation across environmental histories, not uncertainty in empirical parameters, numerical truncation, or demographic stochasticity. Dynamic rare-mutant exponents were separately computed along shared resident histories, using accumulated log normalization after burn-in. They were not obtained by averaging frozen eigenvalues or equated to homogeneous finite-horizon rT.

A separately recorded volatility follow-up uses six new histories at each mean switching interval 20, 50, 100 and 200. At tau=3 the mean fixed d=1−d=0 r600 contrast is −.000172, +.000083, +.000498 and +.000501, respectively. Rapid change reverses the sample mean advantage; the intermediate condition has mixed signs. All paths start at +1 with an initially aligned adult majority. These are finite-horizon contrasts with initialization/phase effects, not stationary averages or invasion rates; six histories are insufficient for a universal threshold claim. Full path counts and uncertainty are in volatility.json.

## Robustness and causal limits

Replacing exponential maturation by four Erlang juvenile substages with the same mean changes adult accuracy and confidence persistence. L=1 exactly reproduces the original two-stage computation. The four-phase robustness case is not a deterministic birthday. Its supplementary recovery/debt columns use an absolute .75 threshold and are not directly comparable to the main baseline-relative metrics. The effects show that the variance of developmental exposure matters, so the selected tau is conditional on the maturation-time distribution.

Memory caps H=1,3,6,9, adult correction rates .1,.3,1, reset-at-maturation, public-only storage, no adult updates, feedback availability, no-payoff and no-truth controls were tested. Their outputs are retained, including nulls. Correlated lineage-based testimony is not explicitly modeled; a randomly redrawn adult's marginal accuracy is sufficient for this generator. Repeated source dependence, cultural epistemic rules, cooperation norms, sanctions and parent–offspring conflict remain outside the executed model.

## Derived-measure correction

Final audit found that coarse 5-unit trapezoidal integration of mortality hazards could produce survival probabilities above one. That derived measure was discarded. The reported survival metric now solves the transient juvenile process exactly with final advice held fixed: m/(m+mu_F) for fixed choice, and a killed trust-state operator for RL. It is a conditional newborn probability, not a realized survival estimate in the changing environment. Independent transient integration and probability-bound tests validate it. The correction changes no population trajectory, growth rate, invasion result, or optimum.

## What this says about specialized deference

The simulations demonstrate a conditional benefit of using and retaining adult information with fixed private cognitive accuracy. They do **not** show superiority over an adequately informed, flexible reliability-sensitive learner: the oracle matches high fixed deference in stable conditions and can avoid harmful social information after changes. Advantages over the particular RL comparator can reflect its limited trust state, softmax exploration and feedback/reset rules. They do not establish an advantage over general-purpose RL.

The belief persistence mechanism is accumulation of endorsed evidence plus finite correction, with precedents in cultural inertia and learning-schedule theory. No new elementary mechanism has been established. The coupling and measurements could support a transparent theoretical comparison paper after stronger source-correlation, maturation-distribution, normative and empirical analyses. These results do not establish the evolution of human dogmatism or childhood obedience.
