# V5 unresolved questions and evidence needed next

V5 establishes conditional benefits of acquired culture and a cost-dependent selection result for a specified frozen adult policy. It does not establish a new generic mechanism or a historical adaptation for childhood dogmatism. Existing work on costly information, learning schedules, confidence, cultural filtering and bounded rationality provides close precedents; see literature_review.md.

The strongest remaining distinction is between **economizing on unreliable information** and **authority-specific resistance to correction**. The former can be useful without the latter. A subsequent model should compare a common information budget and computation limit across: fixed inherited conflict override; learned source credibility; Bayesian or approximate change-point inference; and generic confidence-dependent sampling. Confidence should be calibrated against outcome feedback rather than treated as correct because it is large.

Priority tests:

1. **Evidence dependence and causal complexity.** The main load parameter is a rate transformation, and the repertoire companion has independent facts. Correlated teacher lineages, misleading common sources, dependencies among facts, hierarchical rules and changing causal structure could alter the result substantially. Copying several confident adults must not count one ancestral observation several times.
2. **Search and teaching costs.** Confidence-qualified tutor search currently scans a whole cohort without cost. Separate source quality from effective encounter rate by matching accepted-testimony counts, then introduce finite search, teacher capacity and acquisition costs. Confidence can identify old mistakes as readily as old knowledge.
3. **Proper information weighting.** The exact Bayesian benchmark exposes how equal-sized bounded-score updates can erode a strong prior under weak evidence. Compare calibrated finite-memory alternatives at matched storage and computation cost before treating freezing's advantage as distinctive. A universal Bayesian bound under stable independent evidence does not settle changing or deceptive environments.
4. **Architecture evolution.** Adult freezing/auditing were assigned alternatives. A stronger model would derive query choices from an inherited decision policy or learned stopping rule, and test whether resistant behavior develops from childhood social experience without encoding a frozen adult mode. Trait covariance and developmental constraints remain modeling assumptions.
5. **Maturation distributions and cohorts.** The main hazard is exponential. The repertoire cohort model is not an age-structured demographic robustness proof. Compare explicit age distributions or fixed-delay development before translating tau into a childhood length.
6. **Norms and conflicts of interest.** Objective binary facts do not represent cooperation, coordination, sanctions, authority incentives, parent–offspring conflict or moral rules. These require distinct payoffs and cannot be inferred from factual accuracy.
7. **Empirical connection.** A useful behavioral experiment would manipulate private cue noise/access separately from demonstrator confidence and known accuracy, test agreement and disagreement trials, measure private belief and public choice separately, and distinguish temporary misinformation from true rule change. Query/search costs and later correction should be observed rather than inferred. Genetic inheritance and reproductive consequences would require additional evidence beyond a laboratory copying task.

The most plausible paper contribution is the **quantitative causal decomposition**: retained personal knowledge as a strong comparator; separate override/storage channels; source scarcity versus social access; observable tutor-confidence filtering; and a shared-culture selection–abundance disagreement that changes sign when query cost is removed. The elementary ingredients and several qualitative predictions are established. Priority or novelty of the full result has not been demonstrated by this scoped review.
