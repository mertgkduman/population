# V5 questions, hypotheses and identification

The user proposes that acquired adult convictions may protect children from the difficult, noisy process of learning an environment alone. V4 already showed some stable-environment benefit of retained culture; it did not establish a distinct advantage of dogmatism over adequate evidence-sensitive learning.

V5 makes the private-only competitor stronger: it accumulates private evidence and retains it into adulthood. Personal evidence u, held conviction b, public action, query expenditure, adult testimony and objective success are measured separately. Hereditary d and tau do not alter private cue quality or memory capacity within a scenario.

Predeclared hypotheses H1–H6 and the model decisions are preserved in research_contract.md, before broad runs. They predict conditional, not uniformly positive, effects. The accept×override factorial and conflict-only storage were added in response to independent model review before broad runs. Post-main extensions are explicitly exploratory: cost/memory decomposition, confidence-qualified tutor search, an exact Bayesian benchmark, local invasion-gradient refinement and slow-policy replacement.

The primary estimands are:

1. Within-scenario matched deference contrast at fixed tau and adult policy. This holds source-access conditions fixed; adult advice quality is allowed to emerge endogenously.
2. Adult-policy contrast at fixed d,tau,evidence and cost. Frozen conviction is compared with continuous updating and selective auditing; all retain childhood knowledge.
3. Immediate action override versus persistent memory acceptance. Neither channel is equated with the tied-d result.
4. Same observed signed cues under different causes: a real environmental change versus a misleading observation channel. Without truth-linked demographic feedback, non-oracle trajectories must coincide.
5. Conditional rare-mutant fitness in the same resident culture/resources. Isolated N(T) or rT do not establish evolutionary selection.
6. Multi-context knowledge coverage and advice availability. The repertoire companion measures learning rather than demographic fitness; confidence filtering cannot secretly select by actual correctness.

Evidence required for a distinct dogmatism hypothesis would be stronger than any single positive copying result: resistant policies should defeat retention with well-calibrated, cost-sensitive updating under matched information and computation; resistance should arise from a demonstrated acquisition mechanism; and its genetic/cultural transmission and demographic effects must remain separately identifiable. Those conditions are not assumed true here.

No new elementary mechanism is claimed. The literature review maps ordinary information transfer, retained cultural knowledge, costly evidence acquisition and adaptive learning rates to prior theory. The contribution is a reproducible set of mechanism-discriminating contrasts and counterexamples under a shared demographic/cultural environment.
