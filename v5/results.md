# V5 results: information access, retained knowledge and adult conviction

All quantities are hypothetical simulation data. No parameters were fitted to people or animals. Cognitive capacity, cue quality and access are fixed across d and tau within each scenario. Adult advice is generated by earlier learning and cultural transmission.

## The question changes when the private learner also remembers

V4 already showed a stable-environment benefit from retained culture. V5 strengthens the comparator: private-only organisms accumulate evidence, retain their childhood knowledge, and can continue learning as adults. They are not required to forget and start over. The main model separates private evidence u, held conviction b and public choice; two biological stages remain.

The executed program includes 200 pilot trajectories, 7,950 distinct main demographic trajectories (9,750 displayed cells, including repeated inactive oracle d rows), 315 mechanism-factorial cases, 972 source-condition cases, 1,296 random-history records, 96 memory sensitivities and 216 cost/memory cases. Evolutionary analyses include 96 architecture invasion cells, 648 pairwise cells, 216 time-dependent mutant rates, explicit genetic competitions and focused refinements. Separate repertoire experiments comprise 972 original cohort runs and 1,296 confidence-tutor runs on new seeds. Repeated observations and displayed duplicate cells are not independent experiments.

## Your information-access hypothesis receives conditional support

At the main-grid tau nearest3 (3.0737), the retained/updating model gives:

| Evidence scenario | N(400), d=0 | N(400), d=1 | Matched change | Adult accuracy, d=0→1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stable default, cue p=.65 | 3,879 | 5,195 | +33.9% | .8044→.8734 |
| Noisy private cues, p=.55 | 2,714 | 3,806 | +40.2% | .6960→.7735 |
| Sparse private access, access=.2 | 2,815 | 5,224 | +85.5% | .6726→.8388 |
| Both sources slowed by load=5 | 2,815 | 3,198 | +13.6% | .6726→.6881 |

These are within-scenario contrasts, not isolated causal comparisons between different rows. Sparse private access makes adult information comparatively valuable. Slowing both private and social exposure reduces the advantage. The last two scenarios give exactly the same private-only trajectory because their private rates are algebraically equal, yet diverge with deference because social access differs. Thus evidence scarcity and usable cultural access must be measured separately.

## Frozen convictions can help in a stable noisy world

At exactly tau=3, d=1, p=.55, H=4, with query_cost=.02:

| Adult policy | N(400) | Adult accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Continuous private updating | 3,816.8 | .7714 |
| Frozen adult conviction | 4,364.3 | .7910 |
| Confidence-based auditing | 4,479.5 | .8071 |

Freezing improves abundance by about 14.3% versus continuous updating. Auditing performs better still in this default cell. These organisms have the same private-cue accuracy and childhood acquisition rules; policies differ in later sampling.

Under the same auditing rule at p=.55,tau=3, increasing childhood d from 0 to 1 reduces later private-query rate from .14145 to .10284 per adult per time (about 27.3%) while raising adult accuracy. That behavioral difference follows acquired conviction under a common confidence rule; it does not require a genotype-specific adult rigidity coefficient. The rule itself remains an explicit assumption.

The benefit is not entirely sampling-cost savings. At query_cost=0, the same policies give N=4524.1,4765.4 and4958.0, respectively. The freezing-over-continuous-update difference persists across tested H=2,4,6,8, but its magnitude changes. At H2 and positive cost, freezing narrowly exceeds auditing; therefore no universally best adult policy is claimed.

A crucial qualification is the update mechanism. Unit-step bounded scores need not weight weak new cues properly relative to strong accumulated knowledge. In the exact stable-world Bayesian benchmark, a calibrated prior .95 plus 40 independent cues of accuracy .55 yields expected accuracy .950760. The bounded-score update yields .729586. Optimal cost-free calibrated Bayesian updating cannot lower expected decision accuracy in that benchmark. Freezing can beat a misweighted finite-memory update rule; this is not evidence that refusing useful information beats all rational learning. Real information dependence, costs and uncertainty about environmental change remain relevant departures from that benchmark.

## Current obedience and cultural storage have different effects

The primary d changes both current action substitution and memory acceptance. The predeclared accept×override factorial isolates them. At noisy p=.55,tau=3:

| Channel enabled as d changes0→1 | N(400), d=0→1 | Delta r400 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Both action and storage | 2737.7→3816.8 | +.0008307 |
| Action override only | 2737.7→2598.9 | −.0001301 |
| Persistent storage only | 2737.7→3515.0 | +.0006248 |
| Neither | unchanged | 0 |
| Storage on disagreements only, plus action override | 2737.7→3117.7 | +.0003249 |

Action-only copying improves mean current public accuracy by .0503 but reduces final adult accuracy and abundance. Different survival filtering and cultural feedback matter; current accuracy is not a sufficient population-performance summary. Much of the positive tied-d result therefore comes from retained cultural acquisition, not a demonstrated independent benefit of overriding one's own judgment.

Conditional selection adds nuance. An action-only d=1 mutant in a d=0 retained resident has invasion rates +.001464 in the default stable case, −.001189 in the noisy case and +.000209 in the sparse case. The same mutant is disadvantaged in the tested d=1 cultural resident. These rates use shared resident teachers/resources. Lower homogeneous abundance is not automatically negative invasion fitness, and a benefit is not universal across cultural environments.

## Misinformation and real change reward different responses

At tau=3.0737,d=1, integrated adult-accuracy deficit relative to each policy's matched stable trajectory is:

| Disturbance | Continuous update | Frozen | Audit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Actual truth reversal | 8.731 | 12.194 | 10.778 |
| Temporary misleading private channel | 47.009 | 27.022 | 38.875 |

The disturbance severities are not identical, so these numbers are policy comparisons within each row, not an isolated causal comparison between rows. Freezing protects old knowledge during misleading input, but delays adaptation when truth changes. Frozen adults are eventually replaced by newly matured adults; a frozen individual does not imply a permanently frozen population.

A separate identification control makes signed cue histories exactly the same while changing their interpretation as misinformation versus real environmental change. Without truth-linked demographic filtering, non-oracle cognitive trajectories match exactly. Learners cannot identify the cause from indistinguishable observations alone. The random-history comparison retains truth-linked survival/productivity, so population histories may diverge through that additional feedback. Its intervals describe variation across externally generated histories, not demographic stochasticity or empirical parameter uncertainty.

## Actual fact repertoires and formed adult convictions

The discrete-cohort companion represents 4, 16 or 64 distinct binary facts, rather than calling a reduced sampling rate “complex reasoning.” Founders acquire information through private samples; later adults transmit what they learned as juveniles and adults. Private learners retain childhood knowledge. Six paired seeds per cell support the original grid; six fresh seeds support the tutor follow-up.

With random teachers, the mean stable d=1 versus d=0 accuracy gain averaged across the tested p/policies falls from .1518 at 4 facts to .0691 at 16 and .0145 at 64. Larger repertoires dilute both private and adult knowledge. Complexity does not automatically make copying more valuable.

The exploratory tutor follow-up selects only by observable conviction |b|>=2, never by actual correctness. At16 facts, filtering raises stable d=1 accuracy by about.049–.165 across tested cells; at 64 facts, by .026–.077. This directly tests copying adults with formed convictions. However, cost-free search across the entire cohort also makes rare knowledgeable tutors available: at 64 facts only7–9% of adults qualify, but search finds one in about 99% of encounters. Gains combine source quality and availability, not discernment alone. Search and teaching costs remain omitted.

Confident tutors can also preserve obsolete beliefs. In the D4,p=.55,d1 truth-flip condition, filtering changes final accuracy by −.0988 for frozen adults and −.0417 for auditing, while continuous updating gains .0433. Confidence is a useful cue in some environments and a misleading one after change. These repertoire outcomes are learning measurements, not demographic or invasion fitness.

## Maturation and selection

Within the main homogeneous maps, the best deference is generally d=1, a boundary. The neutral scenario is flat in d and the oracle's d is inactive; neither has a unique deference optimum. Refined abundance maxima include retained stable tau≈1.591, frozen stable≈2.837, audit stable≈1.883, and retained sparse≈3.974. Retained noisy abundance maximizes at the lower primary tau boundary .5.

The rare-mutant gradients give different candidates for retained d=1: tau≈.756 in the default stable case and≈1.055 in the sparse case. No tested mutant among 225 sampled alternatives had positive invasion at each numerical candidate. This is finite-domain evidence, not an analytical ESS proof. In the noisy case, a candidate≈.222 appears only after explicitly expanding the domain below .5; within the primary domain, shorter maturation is favored at the lower boundary.

Explicit architecture competition demonstrates an additional cost-dependent selection–abundance disagreement. In the noisy audit resident, a frozen mutant has invasion +.00011757 with query_cost=.02 but −.00102777 with zero query cost. Starting the frozen lineage at 0.1% under positive cost, a shared-culture, zero-mutation run reaches 98.25% frozen by T=150,000, while total abundance falls from 4479.5 to 4366.6. This is observed near-replacement over a long deterministic run, not literal extinction or a proof of global convergence. Adult architectures were specified as inherited alternatives in this experiment; their appearance was not generated spontaneously from juvenile deference.

## Interpretation

The experiments support the possibility that retained adult information helps juveniles when direct access is unreliable or scarce. They also exhibit a narrower stable-world benefit of freezing acquired convictions under particular cognitive and sampling constraints. Stronger claims fail important controls: current override alone can be harmful; selective auditing can outperform freezing; adequate Bayesian updating removes a cost-free accuracy penalty from valid evidence; and confident source selection can amplify obsolete culture.

The defensible contribution is a quantitative decomposition of acquisition, current deference, retention, query cost, confidence-based source access and demographic versus individual selection. These mechanisms have substantial precedents. No distinct historical adaptation for human childhood dogmatism, universal benefit of adult authority, or genetic independence from intelligence has been established.
