What can count as an equilibrium?
At κ=0, fixed truth permits a demographic-cultural equilibrium. Opposite truth signs give mirrored belief equilibria and equal abundance under this symmetric model. At κ>0, ongoing shocks produce fluctuating responses; the displayed post-burn-in means are estimates over times 200–600, not proof of a unique stationary distribution. The map does not change with the animation clock.
High inherited deference is not directly rewarded. Adult retention can preserve useful information or delay correction. A lower adult encoding probability is an imposed architecture. An optimum at the displayed grid edge is a boundary result; tied cells are reported as a range. Stochastic comparisons use eight matched paths; independent held-out histories are retained in the raw experiments.
Model and scientific limitations
Each inherited trait pair has two stages over two finite cognitive ledgers: private evidence u and held belief b. Private cues update both; accepted adult testimony updates only b. Newborns start neutral and inherit d and τ, not factual content. Adults advise from b and learn privately at the reduced encoding hazard ηAνA. Births depend on adult behavioral correctness and Monod-type resources; error-associated mortality depends on actual decisions. Maturation moves F/τ to A with its existing cognitive state. Thus dN/dt=B−D. No direct benefit is assigned to deference or prolonged maturation.
Deference here bundles current action substitution and social memory acceptance as in V5. The model does not isolate a special dogmatism benefit beyond information transfer, and does not establish a childhood adaptation. Capped unit-step scores are not calibrated Bayesian learning. Environmental flips are symmetric and do not change resource supply. Survival filtering, juvenile replacement and continuing adult updating all affect adult accuracy.
